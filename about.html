About

An independent platform for research at the intersection of sustainability and Nigerian capital markets.

Why this exists

Nigeria has a growing body of ESG disclosure and a growing number of ratings, but very little work connecting either to the way capital is actually allocated. Investors allocate against benchmarks. Until sustainability data reaches a benchmark, it stays a compliance exercise.

The Initiative was set up to close that distance: to test, in public and with reproducible methods, whether sustainability performance carries information a Nigerian investor can use, and to build the benchmarks that would let them use it.

How the work is done

Methods are published before results are promoted. Data sources are named. Robustness tests are reported whether or not they flatter the headline. Where a finding rests on a judgement call, the paper says which call and what happens if it goes the other way.

Independence

The Initiative does not manage money, does not sell ESG ratings, and takes no fees from the issuers it studies. Constituent selection relies on ratings that are already public and produced by a third party with no role in this research. The conflict-of-interest statement is published with each paper.

Who it is for

Pension fund administrators, asset managers, the exchange and its regulators, development finance institutions, and academic researchers working on frontier-market sustainable finance.
